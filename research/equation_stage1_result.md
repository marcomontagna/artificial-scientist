# Equation discovery v0 — result

**The recovery screen failed.** Across 20 seeds per family, the sparse fitter selected the correct terms in 20/20 affine and 15/20 quadratic datasets. It selected nonzero formulas in 9/20 pure-noise datasets (45%; descriptive Wilson 95% interval 25.8–65.8%), exceeding the fixed limit of 8/20. Quadratic false inclusions occurred in 5/20, within that separate limit. No settings were changed afterward.

The large-mismatch adequacy screen passed: fresh observations rejected all 20 sine and 20 exponential sparse candidates, with no rejections in the affine, quadratic or noise families. But the full quadratic baseline rejected only 1/20 exponential approximations, despite mean extrapolation MSE 0.99146. Its interpolation latent-mean MSE was 0.00366 and noisy audit MSE 0.00659. **A good local fit does not establish a globally correct law.** The sparse exponential failure also reflects its three-term cap.

The model searches 42 combinations of six supplied monomials and fits their coefficients. It produces explicit formulas but does not invent operators, variables or physical concepts. Supplied inputs, strong coefficients and known Gaussian noise make this a limited validation study. Heuristic model weights are not calibrated confidence, and no novelty is claimed.

One full run from clean `c0aa246` completed 100 datasets, 400 method fits and 4,000 observations (24 training + 16 audit per dataset). All 74 tests passed before sampling. Internal runtime: 0.313633 seconds; external process wall: 0.344874 seconds; artifacts: 1,870,359 bytes. A distinct reviewer independently reproduced all 4,500 stored candidate/baseline fits and metrics, with maximum discrepancy 1.46e-11. [Compact evidence](../results/equation_discovery_v0) includes the first two declared seeds across every family, without outcome selection.

**Decision:** preserve the failure and defer active acquisition and grammar revision. Claude agreed. The next two studies test independent evidence for individual terms and then challenge a fixed full polynomial across domains. Neither changes this fitter or converts its failed screen into a pass. A rough independent-null heuristic predicted approximately 37% nonzero selection; 9/20 is therefore unsurprising and does not indicate a software bug. The 40% screen was a practical tolerance, not a calibration guarantee.
