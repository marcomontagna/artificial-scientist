# Three candidate gaps — decision research

Author: Codex primary, 2026-09-25. All novelty is unproven. Source references: [initial map](prior_art.md), [focused check](focused_prior_art.md), [independent critique](critique.md). These are three candidate questions, not three discovered gaps.

## 1. Diagnostic sensing after hidden changes

**Question/hypothesis:** With the same adaptive predictor and observation budget, adaptive diagnostic sensing reduces policy-independent post-change log loss relative to uniform observation without material stationary degradation. **Null/falsifier:** no practically meaningful reduction, or the benefit disappears with a matched change-aware predictor and forced exploration.

**Closest work:** [Bandit Quickest Changepoint Detection, 2021](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/f3a4ff4839c56a5f460c88cce3666a2b-Abstract.html); [controlled sensing, 2023/2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17223); [UCB controlled sensing, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28563). Observation selection for detection is already solved in important specified settings. A possible evaluation difference is persistent predictive loss rather than stopping-time delay under false-alarm constraints; changing the metric does not by itself establish novelty.

**Strongest counterargument:** this is controlled sensing with renamed outputs. **Minimal environment:** three binary sensors and hidden distribution switches. **Baselines:** matched adaptive filtering with uniform/cyclic sampling and exploration, plus relevant published detection policies if making detection claims. **Confounders:** privileged post-change law, known change time, unequal samples, scoring only chosen actions, comparing against stale cumulative estimates. **Cost:** small CPU simulation, no model training. **Verdict:** retain as control and motivation; reject broad novelty.

## 2. Predictive information versus parameter information under wrong assumptions

**Question/hypothesis:** In a fixed finite-hypothesis learner exposed to hidden changes, does target-weighted predictive information acquisition (EPIG) reduce post-change target-distribution log loss by at least 0.01 nats per observation relative to both parameter-information acquisition (EIG) and uniform sensing, with stationary penalty no more than 0.01? Does this survive an explicitly misspecified likelihood family? **Null/falsifier:** either comparator fails the threshold or the stationary bound; inconclusive intervals do not support a benefit.

**Closest work:** [EPIG, 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v206/bickfordsmith23a.html), [active learning bias, 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13698), [R-IDeA, 2026](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v300/tang26d.html). Predictive acquisition and robust active design are existing methods. The possible gap is a transparent stress test separating acquisition choice from identical change filtering and exposing tradeoffs across matched/misspecified and stationary/switching conditions. No claim this combination is unpublished.

**Strongest counterargument:** an expected consequence of known EPIG advantages and misspecification failures in a hand-designed toy. **Minimal environment:** three query actions, four known candidate probability vectors, one optional hidden shift; evaluate predictions over a fixed distribution independent of queried actions. **Baselines:** same filter with uniform, EIG, EPIG, EPIG plus 10% uniform exploration; cyclic control; per-action windowed Beta predictor with uniform queries as model-flexibility control. **Confounders:** target distribution favors the selected objective; arbitrary law table; tuning on test shifts; reporting only favorable conditions; misspecification outside the finite model cannot be repaired by better sensing alone. **Cost:** exact sums over four hypotheses and binary outcomes, a few thousand short episodes; benchmark locally before expanding. **Verdict:** select as a replication/stress-test package, not a new method. Detailed protocol in experiments/selected_experiment.md.

## 3. Representation revision versus ordinary parameter adaptation

**Question/hypothesis:** When sensor mixing changes but causal mechanisms remain fixed, updating a learned representation yields better held-out prediction than retraining only a matched-capacity predictor under equal data and compute. **Null/falsifier:** improvement vanishes after matched adaptation capacity, replay and compute; or latent recovery is not identifiable under the allowed observations.

**Closest work:** [COREP, 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/zhang24ah.html), [causal representation review, 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11107), [identifiability cautions, 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.12359), plus world models/I-JEPA in the initial map. Learning useful latents under nonstationarity is not an untouched problem.

**Strongest counterargument:** giving one method a trainable encoder while freezing the other's input transform manufactures the advantage. **Minimal environment:** two-dimensional latent linear dynamics observed through changing invertible mixing, with interventions specified; include fixed, learned and privileged-latent controls. **Baselines:** jointly updated predictor/encoder, fixed encoder with equally flexible predictor, replay/forgetting, and labeled latent oracle. **Confounders:** non-identifiability, coordinate relabeling, hidden-state leakage, unequal parameter count, optimizer effects. **Cost:** materially higher design/tuning burden; optional ML backend only after a justified design. **Verdict:** defer; scope is too ambiguous for tomorrow's first implementation.
