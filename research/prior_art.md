# Initial prior-art map

Research date: 2026-09-25. Author: Codex research worker. Status: bounded first-pass review, pending independent review. This is not a systematic literature review or a claim of novelty. No algorithms were implemented in this phase.

## Conclusion for the research director

None of the broad ingredients establishes novelty: uncertainty estimation, sequential information-gain design, explicit hypotheses, learned representations, causal modeling, and adaptation to changes all have substantial prior work. The present binary changing-law scaffold is a useful control, not a new learning paradigm. A positive comparison against only a cumulative estimator would be especially weak: it would mainly demonstrate the familiar value of forgetting outdated observations.

The most useful next question is a **candidate for investigation**, not a demonstrated gap: under an equal sampling budget, does choosing diagnostic actions improve post-change probabilistic prediction when changes are difficult to observe under the learner's usual actions? This requires a matched change-aware baseline, stationary costs, misspecified priors, and policy-independent evaluation queries. It is close to active change detection, dual control, nonstationary bandits, and Bayesian design; those literatures must be searched before claiming a contribution. A replication/control should precede any new method.

## Four closer comparisons

### P01 — change hypotheses are already a Bayesian baseline

**Setting/information:** sequential observations, assumed independent within segments, with independently drawn segment parameters. The learner has a likelihood, parameter prior, and change hazard; it does not receive true change times. **Budget:** passive observations, no action selection. **Comparison/evidence:** the paper demonstrates three real data sequences rather than the matched intervention benchmark proposed here. **Overlap:** explicit hypotheses over change timing plus predictive uncertainty. **Difference to investigate:** action-dependent observability and misspecification; neither is established as a new gap by this reading. **Baseline consequence:** include a correctly implemented, development-tuned change-aware predictor, not just cumulative and windowed estimators. **Code/license:** original implementation and code license not verified in this pass. [Method and Algorithm 1](https://arxiv.org/html/0710.3742v1).

### P06 — information gain and adaptive design are not new

**Setting/information:** model-based Bayesian design with policy input comprising previous designs and observations. **Budget:** finite design horizon; the location-finding evaluation includes 30 designs. **Baselines:** random, optimized fixed, and sequential variational design. **Overlap:** choosing experiments that distinguish hypotheses. **Difference to investigate:** deployment-time hidden mechanism changes and wrong priors require targeted comparison; the inspected sections do not establish absence of related solutions. **Fairness:** include offline training cost as well as decision latency. A tiny enumerated hypothesis space is preferable to reproducing the network tonight. **Code/license:** [author repository](https://github.com/ae-foster/dad) identifies MIT licensing and PyTorch/Pyro implementation; Apple Silicon compatibility was not tested. [Evaluation, section 6](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/foster21a/foster21a.pdf).

### P07 — distinguish coverage from probabilistic calibration

**Setting/information:** an online predictor receives outcomes and adjusts its target miscoverage parameter. **Budget:** passive sequential observations; no intervention selection. **Baseline:** fixed miscoverage parameter. **Evidence boundary:** long-time average coverage guarantees do not imply coverage at every time step. **Overlap:** reliable uncertainty under shift. **Difference to investigate:** Bernoulli probability forecasts scored by log loss/Brier and reliability are different from prediction sets; adding ACI is not automatically a like-for-like baseline. **Confound:** favorable aggregate coverage can coexist with poor immediate post-change behavior or wide sets. Report local performance and sharpness if set prediction is used. **Code/license:** no author implementation or code license verified. [Sections 2 and 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.00170).

### P09 — intervention optimization differs from discovering a graph

**Setting/information:** the main model assumes a known DAG sparsity pattern and unknown weights in a linear structural causal model; extensions and graph misspecification are discussed. **Budget:** sequential interventions, with samples per intervention; no single universal budget should be transferred to this repo. **Baselines:** random, greedy, and variance-based active criteria. **Overlap:** updating hypotheses and acquiring informative interventions. **Difference to investigate:** hidden mechanism changes under a restricted action budget; not a verified novelty claim. **Confound:** the objective is target-outcome intervention optimization, which differs from graph recovery or broad predictive accuracy. **Code/license:** not verified in this pass. [Model assumptions and comparison sections](https://arxiv.org/html/2209.04744v2).

## Implications and coverage limits

1. A “hypothesis-driven artificial scientist” label does not separate this project from Bayesian inference/design, symbolic regression, or model-based learning. Distinction must be a measurable question, not a name.
2. Require stationary controls and a matched adaptive baseline. If only forgetting helps, report that as replication. Do not claim calibrated uncertainty from lower average log loss alone.
3. Do not introduce latent representations until observations make them necessary. Otherwise representation learning adds confounders and compute while weakening interpretability.
4. Restrict any causal claim to identifiable intervention effects in a specified environment. Passive prediction can validate forecasting but cannot generally identify causality.
5. This pass has **not** covered active change detection, dual control, nonstationary bandits, online calibration/strongly adaptive regret, causal representation identification theorems, active inference, predictive coding, intrinsic motivation, or program synthesis sufficiently. The 2025–2026 frontier is especially incomplete. These omissions block a novelty claim.

The search horizon included 2026-09-25, but this core bibliography ends in 2025.
